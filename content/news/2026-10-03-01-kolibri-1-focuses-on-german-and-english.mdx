Imagine a document assistant that needs to work through German source material and show where each answer came from. That is a more useful starting point for **Kolibri 1** than asking whether it wins a general model leaderboard.

Aleph Alpha released the model on October 3. It focuses on German and English, with open weights under Apache 2.0, explicit reasoning controls, and native tool calling. The company describes a German-oriented tokenizer and training approach. Its October 5 [announcement](https://aleph-alpha.com/en/news/kolibri-sovereign-ai-made-in-germany/) explains that language focus and the documented training-data governance measures.

The [official model card](https://huggingface.co/Aleph-Alpha/Kolibri-1) lists **78B total parameters, with 3.46B active per token**. Activating fewer parameters reduces computation per token; the full model still needs to live in memory. The listed FP8 weight footprint is roughly 78 GB, before allowing for the rest of a serving workload.

Context needs a similar distinction. Aleph Alpha validates up to **1,048,576 tokens**, but recommends **262,144 or fewer** for serving efficiency and complex tasks. Start with the relevant documents rather than treating the maximum window as a target.

For a German-language RAG workflow, compare answers on documents with known answers and on questions those documents cannot answer. Check citations, abstention, and tool arguments. Open weights give you deployment control; they do not establish accuracy on your material or make hosting free.

Kolibri stays out of our token-cost tools for now. This entry covers the release and its deployment implications without assigning an unverified hosted API price.
