Google now has two Gemini 4 models on its paid API, and for the rest of November they cost the same. **Gemini 4 Carbon** and **Gemini 4 Argon** are both $2 input and $10 output per million tokens until November 30. From December 1, both cost $4 / $20. [Gemini API pricing](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/pricing).

Argon arrived first. Google made it generally available in October, and its model page calls it the efficient Gemini 4 model, with lower token spend per turn than Carbon. Carbon reached a group of cybersecurity defenders in late October, and on November 10 Google opened it to all paying developers. [Announcement](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/gemini-4-carbon-now-available/).

Google's own advice splits the two. Argon is the ideal choice for most software engineering and knowledge work. Carbon is for tasks that test the limits of a model, such as complex, multi-step debugging and open-ended research, and it uses more tokens than Argon to explore and verify.

That matters more than the rate. Equal per-token prices do not mean equal bills: if Carbon uses more tokens on the same job, it costs more. The useful comparison is tokens per completed task on one job you can check.

Both models take text, images, video, audio, and PDFs, and return text. The context window is 1,048,576 tokens, shared between input and output, and output stops at 65,536 tokens by default. Neither is on the free tier. [Model documentation](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/models/gemini-4-carbon).

On this site, both join the [model tools](/chapters/reasoning-vs-fast). The estimates switch to the December rate on December 1. Gemini 3.1 Pro stays, because the local benchmark results come from it, and from December it is cheaper per token than either Gemini 4 model. Neither Gemini 4 model has local results yet; both are marked “not tested.”
